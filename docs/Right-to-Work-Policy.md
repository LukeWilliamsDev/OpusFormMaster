# Right to Work Policy

**Opus Form Ltd** (Company No. 17228356)  
**Document:** OF-POL-07  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Approved:** July 2026  
**Next review:** July 2027

## 1. Purpose

Opus Form Ltd must prevent illegal working where it employs or directly engages an individual to carry out work in the UK. This policy explains how we carry out and record those checks fairly and consistently.

This policy supports, and does not replace, the Home Office right-to-work guidance. The latest government guidance and prescribed documents must always be followed where it changes.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to directors, employees, agency workers, labour-only subcontractors, self-employed operatives, consultants and other individuals who will personally perform work for Opus Form Ltd or on its behalf, where Opus Form is responsible for the engagement.

Where a subcontractor or labour agency supplies its own workers and remains responsible for their engagement, that supplier must complete the required checks and provide written assurance when requested. We may request evidence or carry out proportionate assurance checks. If Opus Form directly employs or engages the individual, we do not rely on supplier assurance instead of completing our own check. A tax status, invoice or subcontracting arrangement does not establish a right to work.

Genuine self-employed status does not, by itself, determine whether a right-to-work check is needed. The responsible manager must decide who is engaging the individual and who controls the work, and escalate uncertainty before work starts.

## 3. What proves a right to work

Before work starts, the responsible administrator must complete a compliant check using one of the following routes:

- an eligible original document from the Home Office prescribed-document list, checked in the worker's presence (or by an approved compliant alternative process); or
- a Home Office online right-to-work check using the worker's GOV.UK share code and date of birth, with the result saved and the photograph compared with the person presenting for work; or
- the relevant Home Office Employer Checking Service process where the worker cannot provide the usual evidence and a positive verification notice is obtained.

The check must establish the person's identity, permission to do the role and any expiry date or conditions. A copy of the evidence and the date of the check must be kept securely with the compliance record.

For a manual check, the administrator must:

1. obtain the required original document(s) from the current Home Office prescribed list;
2. check that the document appears genuine, belongs to the person presenting it, and permits the proposed work;
3. check any expiry date, work restriction or conditions; and
4. record the checker's name, the date of the check, the document details and the prescribed wording or applicable verification reference.

For an online check, the administrator must use the GOV.UK employer service, enter the share code and date of birth, confirm that the photograph matches the person presenting for work, and save the profile page showing the result and any restrictions. A screenshot or document supplied by the worker is not a substitute for checking the result through the official service.

## 4. Documents and numbers that are not proof

The following do **not** establish a right to work on their own:

- a CIS registration or CIS number;
- a UTR (Unique Taxpayer Reference) number;
- a National Insurance number, payslip, invoice or proof of self-employed tax status;
- a CSCS card, CSCS registration or CSCS online card check;
- a driving licence, bank statement, company registration or reference from another person.

In particular, a CIS number or UTR relates to tax administration, not immigration permission. It must never be accepted as a substitute for a statutory right-to-work check.

## 5. CSCS and competence checks

CSCS must be treated as a separate competence and site-access check. Where a role or client site requires it, we will record the card number, card type, occupation, expiry date and the result of the official CSCS Smart Check or other current official verification route.

A CSCS card check may help confirm identity, training, qualifications and site access requirements, but it does **not** prove that a person has permission to work in the UK. Both checks are required where both are relevant:

1. right-to-work check; and
2. CSCS, training and competence check.

The CSCS check must not be used to decide a person's immigration status, and a right-to-work check must not be treated as evidence of competence.

## 6. Timing and follow-up checks

- No person may start work until the required check is complete and recorded, unless the Home Office has confirmed a lawful exception or verification route in writing.
- Where permission is time-limited, the administrator must record the expiry date and arrange a follow-up check before permission expires.
- A follow-up check must be completed using the current prescribed process. The original check must not simply be treated as continuing indefinitely.
- If permission cannot be verified, has expired, or contains conditions the proposed role does not satisfy, work must not start or must be paused while the matter is escalated.
- A person must not be allocated work outside the hours, role, employer or other conditions recorded in their permission.

## 7. Fairness and data protection

Checks are carried out consistently for everyone, regardless of nationality, colour, race, ethnic or national origin, religion or belief, sex, or any other protected characteristic. We do not make assumptions based on appearance, accent or name, and we do not request more evidence from one person than the lawful process requires from another in the same situation.

Right-to-work evidence is personal data. It must be uploaded only to the approved private compliance system, accessed only by authorised staff, and shared only where necessary for a lawful operational or site-access purpose. The Staff Privacy Notice explains the applicable data uses and rights.

We retain the check record for the duration of the engagement and for at least two years after it ends, or longer where another documented legal or regulatory requirement applies. Records must then be securely deleted or disposed of in line with the retention schedule. We do not retain unnecessary copies of tax documents or unrelated identity information merely because a right-to-work check was required.

## 8. Responsibilities

- **Managing Director:** owns this policy and ensures resources and oversight are available.
- **Primary Administrator/Dispatcher:** obtains, checks, records and monitors evidence; controls access to compliance records.
- **Site and operations leads:** do not allocate work to a person whose required check is missing, expired or restricted.
- **Workers and contractors:** provide accurate evidence, disclose changes affecting their permission to work, and do not upload altered or misleading documents.
- **Agency and subcontractor suppliers:** provide accurate assurance and cooperate with reasonable verification requests.

The person completing a check must not approve their own exception. Any failed, unclear or late check must be escalated to the Managing Director before work is allocated.

## 9. Monitoring and assurance

The Primary Administrator maintains a register of checks, expiry dates, follow-up actions and exceptions. At least quarterly, the Managing Director or a delegated reviewer samples completed records to confirm that the correct route, result, date, restrictions and retention action were recorded. Any missed check or expired permission is treated as a compliance incident and corrective action is recorded.

## 10. Concerns and escalation

Suspected forged documents, illegal working, exploitation, coercion or modern slavery must be reported immediately to the Managing Director at [admin@opusform.co.uk](mailto:admin@opusform.co.uk). We will handle reports sensitively, protect people from retaliation for raising genuine concerns, and seek specialist or official advice where needed.
